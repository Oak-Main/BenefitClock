What you can — and can’t — change

Annual Enrollment is Medicare’s main change window. Here is what is on the table, and what is not.

✓ What you can do

  • Switch Medicare Advantage plans — one MA plan to another, or from Original Medicare to an MA plan.
  • Switch Part D prescription drug plans — drop your current drug plan and join a different one.
  • Return to Original Medicare — drop an MA plan and go back to Parts A and B.
  • Enroll in Medicare Advantage for the first time, if you already have both Part A and Part B.
  • Add or drop Part D coverage — though dropping it can mean a late-enrollment penalty if you rejoin later.

✗ What you generally can’t do

  • No guaranteed-issue Medigap outside your initial enrollment period. Medigap (Medicare Supplement) insurers can require medical underwriting outside your 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment (the month you turn 65 and are enrolled in Part B) or certain protected situations — meaning they can decline you or charge more based on your health. A Medicare Advantage switch during AEP is not a Medigap right.
  • You can’t use AEP to change Medigap plans freely. If Medigap is your question, check the rules before you drop anything — the decision that protects your MA switch can strand your supplement options.

The long version, with the edge cases, is in the AEP guide.
