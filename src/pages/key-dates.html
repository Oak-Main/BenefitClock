What you can change during AEP

The Annual Enrollment Period ({{AEP_WINDOW_RANGE}}) is the one time every year when almost any Medicare change is on the table. Here's what you're allowed to do:

  • Switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage. Move from Original Medicare (Parts A and B) into a Medicare Advantage plan, or leave Medicare Advantage and go back to Original Medicare.
  • Change from one Medicare Advantage plan to another. If a different plan in your area fits your doctors, drugs, or budget better next year, you can move to it.
  • Add, drop, or switch your Part D drug coverage. Join a stand-alone Part D plan, drop the one you have, or switch to a different one. Comparing drug plans each year matters — nationwide, stand-alone Part D plan choices fell from 474 in 2025 to 367 in 2026.

Any change you make during AEP takes effect {{AEP_COVERAGE_START_LONG}}. Your current plan keeps running until then.

AEP and the MA OEP are not the same window

AEP ({{AEP_WINDOW_RANGE}}) is open to everyone with Medicare, and you can make as many changes as you like — only your last choice counts. The Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period ({{AEP_MA_OEP_RANGE_LONG}}) is different: it's only for people already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, and it allows just one change — switch to another MA plan, or drop back to Original Medicare (and add a Part D plan). If you're on Original Medicare on {{AEP_COVERAGE_START}}, the MA OEP is not for you.

How the two big numbers connect

The reason this calendar feels crowded is that your raise and your plan are decided in the same few weeks. Your COLA depends on inflation measured over the summer; your Medicare choices depend on files CMS publishes in early fall. By the time you're comparing plans in November, you already know your raise — so you can weigh both at once.

Two things decide what actually reaches your bank account: the COLA itself, and your Medicare Part B premium, which is usually withheld straight from your Social Security check. When Part B rises, it quietly claws back part of the raise. That's the gap our COLA calculator is built to show you.

What if I miss the Annual Enrollment Period?

If you miss AEP ({{AEP_WINDOW_RANGE}}) and your plan is continuing into {{AEP_COVERAGE_YEAR}}, your coverage carries into {{AEP_COVERAGE_YEAR}} with whatever changes your plan made — so it's worth reading your ANOC even if you plan to stay put. A plan that isn't renewing is different: if your Medicare Advantage Plan, Medicare drug plan, or Medicare Cost Plan's contract with Medicare isn't renewed, Medicare gives you a Special Enrollment Period from {{AEP_NONRENEWAL_SEP_START}} through the last day in February to switch to another plan, and you'll be enrolled in Original Medicare if you don't join another Medicare Advantage Plan before your current plan ends. See Medicare's official Special Enrollment Periods page. If you're already in a Medicare Advantage plan, you get one more chance during the MA Open Enrollment Period, {{AEP_MA_OEP_RANGE_LONG}}, to switch plans or return to Original Medicare. Outside those windows, you generally need a Special Enrollment Period, which is triggered by specific life events like moving. Check your options on the official Medicare Plan Finder or by calling 1-800-MEDICARE.
